# PoCabinet

- **PoCabinet** (third-person arcade racer — the cockpit view and `cockpit.js` are gone; modes are 1player / multi / demo only, since 2P was removed 2026-09-29 and a stale `/2player` link plays 1P; always daytime, so every night-only effect was deleted, not idled; `Features/PoCabinet`, `Games/PoCabinet`, `js/pocabinet/`). Track geometry lives once, in `Shared/Games/PoCabinetTrackGeometry` (knots, width, atmosphere): the server sim, the in-browser solo race, the scene and the minimap all resample the same knots. Until 2026-09-23 the client drew ellipses while the server simulated splines, the server sim never moved a car, and nothing ever created a multiplayer race. Physics is **two mirrored implementations**: `PoCabinetTrack` / `PoCabinetPhysics` / `PoCabinetAiDriver` (C#) and `track.js` / `physics.js` (JS), with the same constants in the same operation order. Solo races run the JS copy. Multiplayer runs the C# copy on the server while the browser predicts its own car with the JS copy, replaying the inputs the server has not acknowledged yet (`PoCabinetInput.Seq` → `PoCabinetCarState.AckSeq`). A change to one side must land on the other: a scratch trajectory diff (C# console vs node, 5 cars with contacts) agreed to 1e-8 over 1,200 ticks when they were written. `race.js` owns the frame loop (fixed 30 Hz ticks, render interpolation, remote cars drawn 100 ms behind, recording for the `telemetry.js` chart, replay and the MediaRecorder lap clip); the page is only the HUD. Driver aids and keyboard/gamepad/touch/tilt input (`input.js`) shape the input only, so online they need no server support. There is **one lobby and no join codes** (2026-09-29): the first arrival at `/pocabinet/multi` hosts (track + AI stepper), the next take seats up to 8, and anyone arriving mid-race (or at 8) waits in the lobby group with a null seat, can Watch (spectate), and is re-seated on the next `LobbyState`. Seats key on claim identity (reconnect-safe), AI officials fill free seats, and the lobby reopens with the same roster after a race (that is the rematch). The race and its groups use the fixed internal id `PoCabinetLobbyService.RaceId`. The per-tick snapshot has a 2 KB budget at 8 cars (E2E-API contract), so don't add a per-car field without taking one out. The GFX/sound layer (2026-09-23) is render/audio-only and derives everything from poses the client already draws, so it needs no snapshot field and never touches either physics copy: `postfx.js` (one pass: speed blur, lens rain, flash/slow-mo grade), `sky.js`, `scenery.js` (landmarks, lamps, start gantry, press-pen flashbulbs; seeded per track id), `kerbs.js` (apex kerbs are feel only — buzz, shake, rumble, no grip change), `fx.js` (smoke, sparks, persistent skid marks), and a synthesized `audio.js` (V8 PeriodicWave engine on a virtual gearbox, HRTF rival engines with Doppler). `scene.fx` is the per-frame bag race.js/environment.js write into. The photo-finish slow motion scales how much time feeds race.js's fixed-tick accumulator, solo only — lap times stay on the sim clock. `PoCabinetPage.razor.css` also styles the child components (lobby, selectors, settings, dialogue bubble), so those rules must be `::deep` or they silently never apply. **Stored lap times are server-computed (2026-09-29).** A solo submit carries `Inputs`, the player's per-tick controls (quantized to thousandths in `race.js` *before* they are stepped, packed as base64 little-endian int16 triples), and `PoCabinetLapVerifier` re-runs the whole solo race (the player in grid slot 50 and all 99 rivals, contacts) and stores the lap *it* times; the claimed time is only compared and logged. That makes `PoCabinetLapVerifier.Replay` a third mirror of `race.js` `soloTick`: a change to the solo grid, the clock arithmetic, the AI or the lap bookkeeping must land there too. Multiplayer submits carry no proof; the registry keeps each human's server-timed best lap for an hour (`TakeVerifiedLapAsync`, one use; in memory, and written through to `VerifiedResultStore` since 2026-10-01 so it survives a recycle). A submit with neither gets 422. `tests/Shared/Fixtures/pocabinet-lap-proofs.txt` holds browser-engine proofs with the JS-timed lap (JS and .NET agreed to within 2.4e-9 s when written; exactly, since the sim got its own trig on 2026-09-30); regenerate them, never hand-edit, if either physics copy changes on purpose. It proves a lap is physically possible, not that a human drove it. GFX/sound pass 2 (2026-09-29) is render/audio-only too: `music.js` (adaptive synthesized score on audio.js's bus; layers follow battle proximity and the final lap; `music` setting), exhaust flames from `audio.takePops()`, per-car dents (`cars.js` `addDamage`; reworked into crash damage on 2026-10-01, see the end of this paragraph), wet-road light streaks (`reflections.js`), a grandstand crowd (`scenery.js`; positional murmur and cheers) and a glass gauge whose speed/gear spans race.js writes directly. Those spans are rendered empty so Blazor owns no text node in them, and sibling `@key`s in the race section must not collide (the countdown keys on its bare number). **Playground Marble Run** (`playground`, 2026-09-30) is the fourth track and the one that is not a circuit: the course is the marble run of `models/playground.glb` (the model the marble race uses), point-to-point from the top of the run, round the play structure, off the gutter's end at the drop well, down the slide and into the finish tray. One run, `Laps = 1`, about three and a half minutes. Four user calls the same day shaped it, in order: not a loop on the lawn around the model (the first cut), but the marble track itself; the whole thing scaled up until the cars fit the gutter rather than a wider road laid over it; no barrier walls; and then no gutter walls either, because at that scale they stood eight units tall around a one-unit car and hid the scene. So `scene.js` hides the model's gutter, drop well and marbles and lays an open blue chute in the gutter's place (its floor, same width, same bank, on the model's supports), with two white edge lines; nothing on this track has a wall, and the physics still stops a car at the edge. The scale follows from the second call: the model is drawn at 50x (500 sim units per model metre) because that is where the gutter's 26 cm floor equals this track's physics corridor (`TrackWidth` 74 + run-off, flank to flank). Do not shrink it to shorten the race without also narrowing the corridor. Its knots in `PoCabinetTrackGeometry` are GENERATED from the model's marble path (recentred between the gutter's rims; from `RoadFromKnot` a constant-grade ramp replaces the marbles' free fall onto the slide). The bake script lived in a session scratchpad; hand-editing the knots invalidates the `playground-run` lap proof. What makes it work, all keyed off the track definition rather than the id: `FinishKnot` (the loop still closes through a return link nobody drives, so every wrap in the physics is untouched; the lap ends at `PoCabinetTrack.LapLength` / track.js `lapLength` instead of the loop length, and `CoolDownAt` lets finished cars roll into the tray and park half way down the run-out instead of a cool-down lap), `HiddenFromKnot`/`HiddenToKnot` (the return link is never drawn; `track.drawn(i)`), `RoadFromKnot` (`track.built(i)`: the chute up to it, tarmac and a run-off deck after it; on the chute the edge lines matter, because outside them is run-off that drags like grass), and the render-only `KnotZ`/`KnotBank` (`CenterZ`/`CenterBank` on the static world; race.js `place()` gives every car its height, pitch and roll, and the camera, smoke, skids and shadow frustum follow). The chute is three strips meeting under the lines, not one: a banked strip twists where the bank changes, and a line laid on a wider strip dips through it. Lap length, lap count and the cool-down are in all three sim mirrors (race.js, `PoCabinetSim`, `PoCabinetLapVerifier`). The physics stays 2D: the course crosses over itself six times, so cars on different levels of a crossing can still touch, and a blind nearest-point search can land on the wrong level; that is why the start line is half a metre down the run (the grid is clear of the pass below) and why a net body with no hint is seeded from the server's lap fraction. Scenery on a track with height is the kerbs and the gantry only (the model is the scenery). The playground static world is ~25 KB on a multiplayer join (508 samples), not the few KB of the circuits. It is campaign stage 4 and the final race, so the trophy moved off Press Briefing. **UI/AI consolidation (2026-09-29):** the start is the shared `GameIntro` since 2026-10-02 (tracks, paint and settings are its `Options`; the campaign lives on the track cards — a "locked" stage only means the race doesn't count yet, so every track stays raceable; the card waits for `_loaded`, because a skipped card starts the race from its own first render and the stored settings must be read by then), a compact HUD strip (speed and place live on the gauge and badge), results as the summary of the shared `GameOverModal` since 2026-10-02 (one button out; Watch replay and the one Share menu are secondary buttons in the summary, and Back to results from the replay reopens the modal; Race again and the lobby rematch button are gone), and pause as a `showModal()` dialog whose own Esc-close is cancelled because Esc is `input.js`'s pause toggle. The canvas height is `--pocabinet-canvas-h` (viewport minus header and strip); touch controls, the gauge and the results key off it. Render quality and FOV are no longer settings: `scene.js` steps quality down on a sustained dip under 45 fps. Sector splits come from `race.js` (`OnSectorAsync`, crossings interpolated inside the tick), not from the page. `PoCabinetUiSettings` round-trips through `PoCabinetJsonContext`, so a C# property name is its JS key. Volume, mute, reduced motion and colour-safe colours are not Cabinet settings any more (2026-10-01): they are the app's (the settings sheet; the panel has an "App settings" button), the four properties are gone from `PoCabinetUiSettings`, and `settings.js` `sanitize` no longer reads them from the store, so `masterVolume` is a fixed 0.7 mix level under `PoAudioBus` and the motion and palette come only from `<html data-motion>` / `<html data-colorsafe>`. The Solo / Online / Demo switch is the one on the shared intro card, and the lobby has the shared `InviteLink`. **AI:** `PoCabinetAiService` writes the officials' per-track banter pool (`GET /api/pocabinet/banter`, one structured call per track per day, shared by every player — that cache is the pre-bake) and the post-race debrief (`POST /api/pocabinet/debrief`, numbers from `telemetry.js` `debriefFacts`, server-written prompt). Both run on cheap task keys (`pocabinet.banter`, `pocabinet.debrief`), filter every line through `BannedTerms`, and fall back to `PoCabinetDialogue`'s scripted pools / a rule-based debrief on any failure — never a 500. This reverses the original scripted-only decision on user request. Solo races speak the lines client-side (`race.js` `say`, rate-limited), as text in the dialogue bubble only: the speech-synthesis voices (`dialogue.js` `speak`) and their setting were removed on 2026-10-01 (user call). `pocabinet` needs its `AddGameChatClient` registration: without the keyed `InferenceHealthTracker` the decorated client throws. Local dev can point these at Ollama or Gemini through the existing `PoMiniGames:AI:Provider` switch with no code change. **100 cars and car-shaped contacts (2026-09-30).** A solo or demo race is 99 rivals and the player (`PoCabinetCatalog.SoloCarCount`); multiplayer stays at 8 seats, because its per-tick snapshot is budgeted for 8. The rivals are `physics.js` `soloField()` / `PoCabinetPersonality.SoloField`: the four officials, then 95 field racers whose pace and line come from an index hash (integer hash, then only exact double arithmetic, so both sides agree to the bit), gridded in qualifying order with the player in slot 50 (`SoloPlayerSlot`). Not a reverse grid and not the player last: both were tried, and a hundred cars that have to overtake each other on a 27-second lap is a jam. Contacts are between capsule hulls 39 x 17 (the drawn body), not radius-14 circles: one impulse along the contact normal at the contact point (equal masses, restitution 0.2), split between each car's speed, a sideways `slip` and a yaw `spin` that `Step` bleeds off through the tyres. `slip`/`spin` are body state in both physics copies but NOT in the multiplayer snapshot, so online the predicted car corrects for a few ticks after a hit. `ResolveContacts` takes the track: pairs more than 120 units apart along the road are skipped (the other level of a Playground crossing no longer touches), and so are cars `Parked` in a point-to-point run-out (they would fill it and queue back over the finish; race.js hides them once stopped). The AI (`PoCabinetAiDriver.Decide` / `aiControls`) was rewritten for traffic and is three rules: follow the car in its path no faster than it can brake down to (that car is judged in the car's own frame AND must be ahead on the road, or two converging cars wait for each other forever); take the lane with the longest clear run; never turn in on a car alongside. Its corner speed caps curvature at 1/halfWidth, because the centerlines have kinks tighter than a car (Mar-a-Lago's last bend) that a car cuts across. The grid is as many columns as the tarmac takes (`GridColumns`), rows 50 apart that skip any bend tighter than 1.5 road widths (`GridBack`), projected with a hint, and race distance is `-back` corrected by the projection, not `along - length`. Two geometry changes came with it: Press Briefing's last knot moved from (50, 100) to (-90, 80) (the old one folded the centerline into a radius-8 hook at the line; a lone car's lap is unchanged to 0.03 s), and Playground's `HiddenToKnot` went 126 to 122 so the chute is drawn back along the straight the 1,700-unit grid stands on. Client side: only the ten rivals nearest the camera's car are drawn as full cars; the rest are ONE `InstancedMesh` (`cars.js` `CarCrowd`: body, glass, trim and tyres merged, paint as the instance colour), because a hundred full cars at fourteen draw calls each plus the shadow pass took the frame from 29 fps to 3; six rival engine voices, the HUD snapshot carries the whole field only on the first tick and at the finish (the page takes `_totalCars` as the largest count seen), recorded poses are Float32Arrays, and the results list shows the top ten plus the places round the player. Places were rescaled for a 100-car field: top 10 is a win and gets the fanfare, top 25 clears a campaign stage, top 10 in the final wins the trophy (`PoCabinetCatalog.SoloFrontRunners` / `CampaignPassPlace` / `CampaignTrophyPlace`); `FinalPosition` is accepted 1..100. **The sim's trig is its own.** `track.js` `sin`/`cos`/`atan2` and `PoCabinetTrack.Sin`/`Cos`/`Atan2` are built from + − × ÷, floor and sqrt only, the operations IEEE 754 pins to the last bit, and everything that feeds the simulation uses them instead of `Math.*`. Chrome and node disagreed by one ulp in `Math.cos` sixteen ticks into a race, and a 100-car field is chaotic: by 18 s it was a different race, so the verifier re-timed a clean 24 s browser lap as a 43 s crash. With these, a full Chrome race replays in node and in .NET to the identical double (`PoCabinetLapVerifierTests` now asserts equality, not a tolerance). Never put a `Math.sin`/`cos`/`atan2`/`hypot`/`pow`/`exp` back into `physics.js`, `track.js`'s tables or their C# mirrors; rendering code may use them freely. All of this is in all three sim mirrors, and every lap proof was regenerated. The generator lived in a session scratchpad: dump `BuildStaticWorld` per track to JSON (a file-based `dotnet run` app with `#:project` on Shared), then a node loop over `physics.js`/`track.js` that is `buildSoloCars` + `soloTick` + `lapBookkeeping` with a scripted driver, writing `name trackId wet base64 jsBestLap`. A replay is 100 cars for every tick, O(n²) pairs: about a second for a circuit proof and a few for a Playground run. **Personalities, run-offs, crash damage, body styles (2026-10-01).** A persona gained `Wildness` and `Seed`, and a field racer's traits each come from their own byte of two index hashes, so no trait follows from another. `BrakingAggression` now also sets how far up the road a driver looks for its corner (0.7 to 1.3 s of travel). Cars run off the road through `Lapse` (`PoCabinetAiDriver` / physics.js `lapse`): one draw per 380 units of race distance, live for the first 170 and never off the grid, during which the car aims past the edge of the road on one side and believes in 1.5 to 2.4 times the grip. It is a pure hash of seed and stretch (integer and exact double arithmetic, no state, no RNG), it is in all three sim mirrors, and every lap proof was regenerated (Capitol messy < clean < wild still holds). Believing in more grip alone changed nothing on Capitol, whose corners are faster than the cars, which is why a lapse also moves the lane target. Headless, 100 of 100 still finish on all four tracks with 35 to 65 more run-offs a race on the circuits. Crash damage is two separate things. What you see, on every car: one `onBeforeCompile` hook on all of a car's chassis materials (paint, glass, trim and lights share that car's hit list, so the lamp goes in with the wing) crushes the zone round a hit along the direction it came from and shades the folds from screen-space derivatives, and a hard hit bends the nearest wheel. Full cars are drawn from a `TessellateModifier` copy of the body because the plain one has no vertices between its panel edges; the crowd keeps the plain one. fx.js wall events now carry the speed INTO the wall (a scrape at full speed is a scuff) and land on the part of the body that was facing it. What you feel, on the player's car only: `race.js` `wear` (pull, lock, power) shapes the INPUT after the driver aids and before it is quantized for the proof or sent online, so neither physics copy, the verifier nor the snapshot changed. The rivals only dent, because their controls come out of `aiControls`, which the verifier re-runs. There are five body styles (`cars.js` `STYLES`, picked by car id, the player's is the coupe) on one footprint, so the hull is unchanged, and the crowd is one InstancedMesh per style. Paint is satin, not clearcoat (user call). `/pocabinet/demo` picks a random track every time it starts (the 2-second card, then `StartRaceAsync`), has no results modal, and loops onto another track 8 seconds after the finish (`LoopDemoAsync`). The proof generator was rebuilt from the recipe above and once more lives only in a session scratchpad.
