# PoMarbleRace

- **PoMarbleRace** (`Features/PoMarbleRace`, `Games/PoMarbleRace`, `js/pomarblerace/`). **Board totals are server-recomputed (2026-09-30):** a submit carries the run's races (place, finish time, lead over 2nd) and `MarbleRaceRunVerifier` re-adds them and stores *its* total (422 if it can't: a non-top-10 race, an unknown map, or a finish faster than the course at 2× `MAX_SPEED`). That makes it a second copy of `game.js` `_resolve` — SCORE_TOP, the dominant bonus and the streak multiplier change in both, and a moved finish line changes its `FinishDistance` table. Places are still the client's word (no server physics). The intro card is the only setup screen: `PoMarbleRace.menu()` supplies maps (maps.js is the single source; the page names none), skins/weights (marbles.js) and per-track stats (localStorage) as one JSON string read through `MarbleMenuJsonContext`; after it there is only a 3 s countdown pill whose Change track reopens the card (`GameIntro.ForceShow`). Per-map lighting is a `theme` on the maps.js entry. Heavy tier draws the player and the top three rivals in `MeshPhysicalMaterial` transmission; the ribbon trails are Meshes, so they carry `userData.noAO`, which scene.js hides from GTAO's prepass (it only hides Points/Lines). `audio.js` must never close the shared `PoAudioBus` context. **Maps and staying on them (2026-09-30).** Maps 1 (Neon Chute) and 4 (Canyon Run) are the procedural generator (`track-procedural.js`, Canyon Run = a `variant`); 2 (Spiral Works, hand-modelled) and 3 (Grand Spiral, a bpy script) are Blender GLBs whose baker is gone, so everything below is derived at runtime from the shipped collision shell (4-vertex rings per channel: wall top, floor edge, floor edge, wall top). `track-glb.js` builds per-lane frames from it (`LANES`, `refineLane`) — the baked centerline follows the main lane only and is turned ~90° across junctions, so never judge a marble against it alone. Containment: walls extended to ≥18 with an invisible lid (capped under real overpasses — an overpass is >150 race-units away, not a neighbouring/banked lane), flat caps where a lane starts wider than its feeder, angled guide walls where it ends wider than the next, floor bridges across segment gaps (overlap only — full-width bridges jammed merges), funnel rim walls with gaps at lane ends, no lid for 16 rings after a free fall/funnel plus a 6-high back kerb, Catch lanes walled but never lidded. `sweepGuard` traces every marble's per-frame path against the WHOLE shell + containment (centre crossings are always tunnelling at 85 u/s) and puts it back. game.js retires a marble only when out of envelope AND touching nothing for 1 s, re-acquires one touching track off-envelope, and escalates an unstick nudge (along its lane) for anything gaining <12 units per 5 s. The census/trace scripts that found all of this lived in a session scratchpad; `MarbleRaceMapCertificationTests` (E2E-UI, slow) is the durable check — maps 1 and 4 pass; at last measurement Spiral Works lost 2 of 101 and Grand Spiral 21 (16 through its funnel's throat, which has no chute to Lower A yet). Track picker cards read `made`/`vertices` from maps.js (vertices measured, constant per map) and the world record from `/api/marblerace/records` (one row per map, fastest wins). **Map 5, Playground Run (2026-09-30)** is an imported scene (`models/playground.glb` + `playground_colliders.glb`, a playground export that shipped with its own Rapier reference race, drawn at SCALE 40). `track-playground-path.js` was baked from that reference race: centerline, smoothed frames, per-sample gutter cross-sections, floor heights isotonic-fitted to descend at ≥1.2° so nothing traps. `createGlbCourse` `ringLanes` synthesizes lanes from those rings — the gutter's ring shell IS its collider (cannon-es bleeds a marble from ~100 to ~30 u/s on the tessellated authored gutter's internal edges), plus a containment-only lane walling the bare slide (no back kerb: marbles land on both sides of its first ring); the well, slide, tray and ground collide as authored. The grade is too gentle for cannon-es, so the map declares `driveAccel` (16 u/s² along the course for marbles touching track, game.js `_applyDrive`). Certified 101/101, winner ~58 s. Its bake script lived in a session scratchpad and needs the original export to rerun. **The drawn gutter is posed onto the collider, not the other way round (2026-10-01).** The ring shell was never where the model drew its gutter (the model banks up to 52 degrees, the baked frames are smoothed and nearly flat: floor centre off by up to 2.4 in height and 5 sideways), so half the field was drawn sunk into the floor or floating over it. `track-playground-path.js` `DRAWN` holds the drawn gutter's floor centre and bank per sample, and `track-glb.js` `conformToShell` (the course's `conformNodes`) re-poses the gutter and its supports onto the shell once at load; no collider moves, so the race, the course length and the verifier's `FinishDistance` are unchanged. Moving the collider up to the authored banks instead was tried and rejected: the field pools single file in the low corner (winner 64 to 68 s, three to four times the unstick nudges). The cost is that the authored banks are drawn flattened and the gutter up to 1.24x wider on straights. The same day, for every map: `physics.js` `stepWorld` returns the time actually simulated and `game.js` runs the race clock on it. cannon-es stops sub-stepping once a step costs more than a frame and drops the rest, so on a slow machine the clock outran the marbles and the 180 s failsafe ended a race with a third of the field home; a slow machine now gets a slow-motion race instead. Hit-stop (2026-10-01): `_frame` multiplies the step it asks `stepWorld` for by `PoImpact.getTimeScale()`, so the winner's finish and a player collision above 14 hold the frame for a beat; the race clock runs on simulated time, so finish times and the verifier are unaffected, and the collision one is local races only (online, the host's clock is the guest's race). The photo-finish rack focus fires only when the camera is on the winner (it used to blur the player's own view for three seconds when someone else won).
